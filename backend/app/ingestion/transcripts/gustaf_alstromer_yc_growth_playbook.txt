Lenny: Gustaf, you were Head of Growth at Airbnb and have advised hundreds of top startups as a Group Partner at Y Combinator. What is the single biggest growth mistake you see early-stage founders make?

Gustaf Alströmer: The number one mistake is pouring top-of-funnel acquisition into a leaky bucket. If your retention curve does not flatten parallel to the x-axis, you do not have product-market fit, and any money or effort spent on user acquisition is wasted.

Lenny: How should founders analyze retention curves correctly?

Gustaf Alströmer: When you plot your cohorts (Day 1 to Day 30 for B2C, or Month 1 to Month 12 for B2B):
1. A healthy product has a retention curve that decays initially and then levels off horizontally into a stable asymptote.
2. An unhealthy product has a retention curve that continually slopes downward toward zero.

If your curve goes to zero, you must stop all growth marketing immediately and fix the core activation and value proposition.

Lenny: How should startups choose their North Star Metric?

Gustaf Alströmer: A great North Star metric must satisfy three criteria:
First, it must capture the moment of actual customer value (e.g. for Airbnb, nights booked; for Spotify, time spent listening; for Uber, rides completed). Revenue is a lagging output, not a North Star.
Second, it must be an input metric that teams can directly influence on a weekly basis.
Third, it must align product quality with long-term retention. If your North Star goes up while retention drops, you are optimizing the wrong metric.
